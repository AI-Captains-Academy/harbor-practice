We've spent six lessons making home hosting sound great. Here's the honest counterweight.

Some of you should *not* host at home. And the line isn't mysterious... it's a short list of conditions where the VPS wins again.

## When to graduate from home hosting

**Your power bill exceeds your VPS rent.** A real server at home pulls real watts. If your electricity cost passes $6–$20 a month, just rent the VPS. (The math is that simple.)

**Your ISP upload cap throttles your users.** Slow home internet plus real traffic equals a bad experience for everyone but you.

**You need DDoS protection (a flood of junk traffic meant to knock your site offline) beyond Cloudflare's free tier.** The moment you're paying Cloudflare for better security, you may as well pay for a VPS instead.

**You need a second location for redundancy.** Congratulations... you're running infrastructure now. That's a different conversation.

(But some use cases *never* graduate. Smart home automation, your own AI agents — those are more sovereign at home, full stop. There's no VPS that makes your living-room cameras more private than your living room.)

## The decision matrix

Four real "I want to" statements, four honest answers:

- **"I want to blog."** → VPS + static site. Done in an evening. (You *could* host it at home and probably be fine. I just wouldn't, for anything public.)
- **"I just want to run AI locally."** → You need a really good GPU. This is a hardware problem, not a hosting problem.
- **"I want to share movies with friends."** → Home server *plus* careful legal thinking. (Be careful, Captain.)
- **"I want to learn Linux and sysadmin."** → Both. But *start* with the VPS — cheaper mistakes.

## The three levels (the whole workshop in one frame)

A captain in the workshop nailed this, so I'll use his framing:

1. **Pay a service for the whole stack** — Wix, Squarespace, $20/month. They do everything. You never touch the server.
2. **Rent a VPS** — $20/month. Full server access. You can do more than a website.
3. **Host at home** — $100 Raspberry Pi. Paid for in 5 months. Full ownership.

That's the ladder. Each step trades convenience for control. There's no wrong rung — there's just the rung that fits *your* intention.

(Ron's version, which I keep coming back to: it's a *complexity slider*. The more you do yourself, the more complex it gets — but that's where the learning lives. Sometimes it makes sense to host it yourself, sometimes it doesn't. The judgment is the skill.)

## Proxmox — containers at the OS level

My main home server is a Zima Blade with 16 GB RAM running *Proxmox*.

Like Docker, Proxmox runs containers — but it's an *operating system built for it*. The whole OS exists to slice your machine into virtual machines and containers. Each one maps to different ports.

Here's what's running on mine right now:

**Frigate** — watches the security cameras, runs AI object detection (pigeon vs. cat vs. human), and when the cameras are armed, sends alerts.

**Exit node** — so every Tailscale-connected device still routes through my Mullvad VPN for privacy. Even away from home, my traffic stays private.

**NanoClaw** — my AI systems administrator. The reason I like it here: every task spawns an agent in *its own isolated container*, and the orchestrator checks permissions against a 1Password vault before handing over credentials. No agent gets a key it wasn't granted. (That matters when your agent has access to your home network.)

**Home Assistant OS** — the dashboard for everything. Camera feeds, alerts, AdGuard Home (network-wide ad blocking), media server, energy and solar management. When Frigate detects a person, NanoClaw sends my wife and me a Matrix message with a photo. I reply "send the clip" and watch the full recording.

(That's a *system*. Not one app — a handful of services, containerized, watching each other's backs. This is what home hosting looks like when it clicks.)

## Umbrel — the one-click everything store

*Umbrel* started as a Bitcoin self-hosting OS. Now it's just... everything.

One-click install: **Hermes**, **OpenClaw**, **Jellyfin**, **Nextcloud** (your own Google Drive), **Immich** (your own iCloud photo sync), a Bitcoin node, Lightning channels, **ESPHome** (manage ESP32 sensors — air quality, humidity, smoke), **Gitea**, personal finance apps, even a YouTube downloader.

(Immich also runs on the Umbrel pod. We're working on getting my wife's photos off iCloud and onto our own server. That's a real sovereignty project.)

Cloudron, Proxmox, Umbrel, Home Assistant — they're all variations on the same idea: containerized apps on your own hardware, with a different focus each. Cloudron leans toward public web apps. Proxmox leans toward raw infrastructure control. Umbrel leans toward Bitcoin and personal apps. Home Assistant leans toward your house.

## The AI alternative (one more time)

A captain asked the right question in Q&A: *should I just use AI instead of Cloudron?*

My answer: for simple things, yes. Rent a VPS, install cloud code, tell it to set up Docker and your apps. The tokens are subsidized right now, so it barely costs anything.

But Kenneth added the wisdom that matters more: **identify your routine tasks first, then automate.** Don't build an automation system and then look for things to automate. Figure out the 90% of admin work you actually do repeatedly — *then* decide whether n8n, a script, or an agent is the right tool.

(That's intention-driven development. It's what we're about around here. Decide what you want *before* you build the thing that does it.)

## Where you'll use this

This is the finale. You now have the full picture: VPS for public, home for private and sovereignty-critical, containers for isolation, tunnels for safety, and the judgment to know which tool fits which job. Everything in the main curriculum — the agents, the harnesses, the autonomous systems — runs *on top of* what you've built here.

## Key takeaways

- **Graduate from home hosting** when: power bill beats VPS rent, ISP caps throttle users, you outgrow Cloudflare's free DDoS tier, or you need multi-location redundancy.
- Some use cases (smart home, private AI agents) *never* graduate — they're more sovereign at home.
- **Three levels**: managed service (Wix) → VPS → home server. Each trades convenience for control. No wrong rung.
- **Proxmox** = OS-level containers for infrastructure control; **Umbrel** = one-click app store for Bitcoin + personal apps; **Home Assistant** = your house's dashboard.
- **Intention-driven development**: identify the routine task *before* you build the automation. Don't build first and look for problems later.
