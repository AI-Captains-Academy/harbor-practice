# 🏆 2.5: Capstone — The Same Page on Three Rungs

**No video clip — this lesson is activity-only.** Everything you built/will build across Modules 1 and 2, turned into one decision.

---

You deployed a one-page site. Three times, on three rungs.

Now we break all three... and see what's left standing.

This is the capstone. No new concepts, no new tools. Just the judgment.

## Kill Your Darlings

Each rung taught you something the slides couldn't. Now you prove it by destroying the thing you built.

**Level 1 — let it live.** Don't cancel anything. GitHub Pages or Netlify just... stays. It costs $0, it needs no maintenance, and it'll still be there next year whether you think about it or not. (That's the whole point of Level 1. Set and forget.)

**Level 2 — destroy the droplet.** Log into the provider dashboard. Hit destroy. Watch the final bill land — in pennies, if you used hourly billing and cleaned up after yourself. The cost column of your scorecard just filled in with a real number. (One nickel. Maybe two.)

**Level 3 — pull the plug.** Physically. Walk over to the device, pull the power cable, wait 10 seconds, plug it back in. Feel who owned uptime for those 10 seconds... (You did. Not a provider. Not a status page. You.)

## Complete the Rung Scorecard

Open `rung-scorecard.md` — or the copy you've been filling in across the course.

Now fill in every empty cell. From your deployments. Not the slides, not my numbers, not a guess.

- **Time to live** — how long did each one *actually* take you?
- **$/month** — what did it *actually* cost? (Level 2 in pennies. Level 3 in kilowatt-hours.)
- **What breaks it** — name the one thing that took each rung offline, or would have.
- **Who can take it down** — who held the kill switch at each level?
- **Who sees your IP** — whose log saw your home address?

Every cell needs your data, not a paraphrase of the lesson. If you skipped a rung (never deployed Level 3, say), say so honestly. An empty cell with "didn't do this one" beats a copied answer.

## The Decision Memo

This is the final deliverable. Not a quiz — a memo to yourself.

Pick **2–3 real projects** from your own life. For each one, write:

- **Which rung** it belongs on (Level 1, 2, or 3)
- **Why** — using the 2.4 graduation conditions (power bill vs. VPS rent, ISP upload cap, DDoS needs, redundancy needs)
- **What you'd run it on** — the actual provider, hardware, or platform

Examples to prime the pump (use your own, not these):

- A portfolio site → Level 1. Free, zero maintenance, you'll never touch the server.
- An AI agent that answers your phone → Level 3 (Tailscale, private). Sovereignty-critical. Never put it on a VPS you don't control.
- A payment page with real traffic → Level 2 (VPS). Public, needs reliability, liability stays off your home network.

(Notice the pattern: *public* things go up the ladder only as far as they need. *Private* things come home. The judgment is in the mapping.)

## Where You'll Use This

This is the end of the course — and the start of the real work. Everything in the main curriculum (the agents, the harnesses, the autonomous Pi systems) runs *on top of* one of these three rungs. You now know which rung fits which job. When the main curriculum says "deploy your agent," you'll know whether that means Netlify, a VPS, or a Pi behind a tunnel.

## Key Takeaways

- The same page on three rungs teaches more than three pages on one rung. The *difference* is the lesson.
- **Kill your darlings** — destroying each deployment proves what you learned. The restore button you never pressed is a wish.
- The **Rung Scorecard** is a table of experience, not facts. Every cell from your deployments, not the slides.
- The **decision memo** is the skill: mapping real projects to the right rung, defended with the graduation conditions from 2.4.
- Public things go up the ladder only as far as they need. Private things come home. The judgment is the skill.
